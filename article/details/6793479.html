Front desk flashed a pale digital notice: a key card labeled Limited Time. The clock in the lobby ticked louder as the corridor lights flickered once, twice. I held the card like a promise and an accusation at the same time.

The room numbers around me whispered with echoes of people who had touched these doors before, as if each door kept a trace of a story that never happened. We were told to enter several rooms, to hunt for clues that would fit a shape only the hotel seemed to know.

I asked the receptionist why the records spoke of an unresolved case that never appeared in the guest logs. The response was a quiet shrug, a retreat into the shadowed corner behind the counter.

I step back to a different floor where the same key card glints under a dust of years. A notebook in a room’s desk lists the same room numbers I must visit, but with dates that never match today.

In a faded photograph tucked inside the notebook, a figure similar to me watches the hallway from behind a door, a silent witness to something that should not have happened.

Back in the present, I notice a small, almost imperceptible notch on the door frame where the card’s edge has rubbed over time, suggesting a hidden mechanism rather than a simple lock.

Two teams circle the same corridor, trading not insults but careful, almost ritualistic traps—false lead cards, decoy footprints on the carpet, a routine check that never ends up on any guest log.

I observe a pattern: certain rooms always yield only half-clues, while others reveal a whole picture only when approached with a partner’s timing. If I move when they move, we collide and lose nothing and gain nothing; but if I sidestep, I catch a thread they miss.

A steward’s baton taps rhythmically in the distance, counting down. The air tightens around me as if the building itself is choosing sides.

The first concrete signal appears: a room card with a note that reads as if someone planned to erase a record rather than add to it. On the back, a sentence in shaky handwriting—‘The target is the lure’—and beneath it, a map fragment showing corridors that never appear on the official layout.

Inside the card’s sleeve, a pressed photograph of a room that was never in records but which I distinctly remember visiting in a dream. The dream felt like a memory of someone else's life, not mine.

A door opens to reveal a wall of files. Each file is stamped with a title that doesn’t belong to any case we’ve known: “The Unwritten Entry.” The air carries the scent of rain and iron.

A comparison of the room log and the card fragments reveals a hidden beneficiary—someone who curated what would be seen, and more importantly, what would remain unseen.

The back of the card contains a whispered instruction: “Protect what never happened,” a directive that points to a larger network influencing the very fabric of this hotel’s memory.

As the truth clicks into place, I realize the real game isn’t about solving a past case but about controlling which past gets erased and which past gets preserved.

The final choice is mine: expose the orchestrator behind the erased case, or shield them to keep the fragile balance intact. I weigh the cost against the number of rooms still to be opened and the people who will lose sleep over what I publish.

I decide to leave the hotel’s halls as they are seen by most—silent, orderly, intact—yet I pocket a counter-record, a second card that can unlock a chapter no one else will read.

As I step into the night, the corridor sighs with a quiet relief, and the hotel resumes its routine, unaware that its memory has shifted just enough to matter.
